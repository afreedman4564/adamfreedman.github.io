How I Think

Better decisions don’t always require more analytics.

Most operational performance problems are not data problems. They’re decision system design problems.

I start with the business problem, not the technology. I look for the bottleneck, understand the tradeoffs and implications, and determine what will actually improve the outcome.

Sometimes that means building an advanced analytical model. Sometimes it means redesigning a process. And sometimes the best solution is simply removing a step that doesn’t add value.

The objective isn’t more analytics. It’s better decisions.

Transformation in Action

How I turn complex operational problems into better decisions and simpler ways of working.

01 — From Proof of Concept to Enterprise Capability

Turning forecasting variability into a scalable decision capability with a machine learning foundation

From a focused proof of concept to an enterprise capability.

Challenge

Forecasting performance varied significantly across domains, creating operational challenges and an opportunity to improve decision quality and consistency.

Approach

I led a machine learning proof of concept for new account forecasting in the United Kingdom and Mexico. Rather than optimizing for model complexity, we focused on identifying the approach that best addressed the forecasting problem and could be deployed, evaluated, and maintained at scale. The results demonstrated the potential of the approach and led to an expanded mandate to build a Machine Learning Center of Excellence from scratch.

Impact

The Center of Excellence deployed 43 machine learning models in its first 12 months, creating a repeatable capability for improving forecasting decisions across multiple domains.

02 — Simplifying Before Automating

Removing complexity instead of automating it

Sometimes the best transformation is removing something that doesn’t add value.

Challenge

A process was being prepared for automation, but one existing step would have added unnecessary complexity.

Approach

Rather than automate the existing process as is, I challenged whether the step created enough value to justify keeping it.

Impact

The step was eliminated, simplifying the process and reducing approximately 35 hours of monthly effort.

03 — Deciding with Imperfect Information

Making the best decision with the information available

Good decisions do not require perfect information.

Challenge

Forecasting drivers were inconsistent and conflicting during an annual planning cycle.

Approach

I aligned stakeholders around the best forecast supported by the information available at the time. When the underlying drivers became clearer three months later, I communicated the implications and recommended a re-forecast.

Impact

The updated forecast identified approximately 30 incremental FTE and $2M in incremental operating expense.
